# Visibility-of-Notable-People
Investigating Western Visibility Advantage in Wikipedia’s Record of Notable People

Do western individuals have a visibility advantage when gender, occupation and age are controlled for?
The Cross-Verified Database of Notable People[3] records visibility ranking for over two million figures -
lower ranking means higher visibility. Over 70% of the individuals in the database are of western origin,
thus raising the question of their overrepresentation in the results. This report aims to investigate whether
this advantage persists after protected characteristics, as defined by the UK Equality Act of 2010[7], are
accounted for.
My investigation found that after controlling for age, gender and occupation, there is no western
visibility advantage. Although an initial Mann-Whitney U statistical test found that there is a western vis-
ibility advantage, a linear regression model found that the western bias disappeared after age, gender and
occupation were controlled for. This suggests that the advantage is due to the historical overrepresentation
of western figures in highly visible roles rather than an existing editor bias.
Using interaction models, I found that western women and non-western individuals in certain oc-
cupations have an advantage. Further analysis found that within the same occupational field, western
individuals were less visible than their non-western peers in categories like military, sports and family. As
for gender, western women hold a visibility advantage over their non-western peers, while western men
do not.
Based on the above, in the Wikipedia dataset, there is no western bias. Further research could examine
additional confounding variables to better understand what affects visibility across the dataset.
